## Problem Statement

Mary's Place serves families experiencing homelessness across King County through shelter, mobile outreach, and prevention, but two operational bottlenecks are capping impact at a time when demand far exceeds shelter capacity and average length of stay continues to rise. Intake is manual and siloed: separate health, housing, and youth services teams reassess each family in a clunky ~1990s SQL system, demographics are re-entered by hand into the county HMIS, and the ~50 steps to rehousing have no shared sequencing, no estimated journey length, and no language-aware support — despite 4–8 active languages in shelters at any time (12–15 across a year). At the same time, in-kind donations are abundant but poorly matched to need: only ~15–20% of donated goods are usable by families, the warehouse has no real inventory system, outreach and prevention teams have no visibility into supply, and corporate partners keep funding drives against generic wish lists (e.g., 20 cases of branded coats sitting unused) while families moving into housing receive ad-hoc, day-of setups. Together these gaps mean fewer families sheltered, slower moves into stable housing, and community generosity that does not consistently translate into impact — closing them directly expands Mary's Place's capacity to serve more families without adding shelter beds or fundraising dollars.

## Target Users

### Staff Personas

**1. Case Manager / Housing Specialist**
- **Context:** Acts as central navigator for each family, coordinating handoffs to health, housing, and youth services teams. Works in a clunky 1990s-era SQL system shared across teams.
- **Needs:** A single view of each family's barriers, goals, and next steps; prioritized task lists; less duplicate data entry between internal system and county HMIS.
- **Goals:** Move families through shelter as efficiently as possible without sacrificing quality of care; spend meeting time on the work that matters, not collecting baseline information.
- **Pain Points:** Manual, repetitive assessments across siloed teams; no way to estimate a family's expected journey length; complex 50-step path to rehousing with no built-in sequencing.

**2. In-Kind Donations & Warehouse Staff**
- **Context:** Receives, sorts, values, and distributes physical donations across shelter "marketplaces." Counts inventory by bins (not SKUs) primarily for audit and valuation.
- **Needs:** A lightweight way to know what's on hand, what families need, and where the gaps are; a channel to push usable-but-not-for-us items to partner orgs.
- **Goals:** Get the right goods to shelter, outreach, and prevention families at the right time; reduce waste and storage burden from unusable donations.
- **Pain Points:** ~80–85% of donated goods aren't usable by guests; no inventory system; ad-hoc phone-call orders from outreach staff; no signal back to donors about what's actually needed.

**3. Outreach / Mobile Services Specialist**
- **Context:** Works in the field with families living in cars, tents, or at risk of losing housing. Coordinates flexible funds, move-in support, and material goods.
- **Needs:** Visibility into warehouse inventory and the ability to request household setup kits with lead time; coordination with the "Make a Home" program.
- **Goals:** Help families sustain housing by removing financial and material barriers quickly.
- **Pain Points:** Currently the "squeaky wheel" — has to call the warehouse and grab whatever's available the day a family moves in; no systematic way to surface upcoming needs.

### Family & Community Personas

**4. Shelter Guest Family (Multilingual)**
- **Context:** Family experiencing homelessness in King County, often newly arrived; speaks one of 4–15 languages active in shelters at any given time. Navigating a complex, multi-team intake process.
- **Needs:** Information and intake interactions in their preferred language and cultural context; a clear, paced view of "what happens next" on their journey to housing.
- **Goals:** Regain stable housing; understand and complete required steps without getting lost in handoffs.
- **Pain Points:** Overwhelming volume of assessments and appointments; language and cultural barriers with staff and forms; no visibility into how long their stay might last or why steps are sequenced the way they are.

**5. Outreach / Prevention Family**
- **Context:** Lives in a car, tent, or precarious housing — or is moving from outreach into a new apartment with little or nothing. Served by mobile teams, not in shelter.
- **Needs:** Access to household essentials (kitchen items, linens, beds, microwave) at or shortly after move-in; a way to make their needs visible to Mary's Place.
- **Goals:** Sustain a successful move into housing; avoid sliding back into shelter or unsheltered homelessness.
- **Pain Points:** Today's distribution is hit-or-miss and shelter-biased; needs surface too late or get filled with whatever happens to be in the warehouse that day.

**6. Corporate & Community Donor**
- **Context:** Individuals dropping off household goods, plus corporate partners (e.g., Amazon, REI, Microsoft) running coat drives, meal-kit builds, backpack assemblies, and seasonal giving campaigns. Motivated by visible impact on families.
- **Needs:** Clear, current guidance on what Mary's Place actually needs; easy ways to channel employee/volunteer energy into high-impact activities; feedback that their contribution was used.
- **Goals:** Make a meaningful difference for families experiencing homelessness; strengthen their organization's community presence and employee engagement.
- **Pain Points:** Generic Amazon wish lists don't reflect real-time need; well-intentioned drives generate stockpiles of unusable inventory (e.g., 20 cases of branded coats sitting in the warehouse); no closed loop on whether donations were useful.
